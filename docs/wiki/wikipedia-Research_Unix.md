Original Unix operating system from Bell Labs

**Research Unix** refers to the early versions of the [Unix](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix "Unix") operating system for [DEC PDP-7](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDP-7 "PDP-7"), [PDP-11](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDP-11 "PDP-11"), [VAX](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAX "VAX"), and [Interdata 7/32 and 8/32](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdata_7/32_and_8/32 "Interdata 7/32 and 8/32") computers, developed in the [Bell Labs](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs "Bell Labs") Computing Sciences Research Center (CSRC). The term *Research Unix* first appeared in the [Bell System Technical Journal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_System_Technical_Journal "Bell System Technical Journal") (Vol. 57, No. 6, Part 2 July/August 1978) to distinguish it from other versions internal to Bell Labs (such as [PWB/UNIX](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PWB/UNIX "PWB/UNIX") and [MERT](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-Environment_Real-Time "Multi-Environment Real-Time")) whose code-base had diverged from the primary CSRC version. However, that term was little-used until Version 8 Unix (1985), but has been [retroactively](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retroactive_continuity "Retroactive continuity") applied to earlier versions as well. Prior to V8, the operating system was most commonly called simply UNIX (in caps) or the UNIX Time-Sharing System.

**Ancient UNIX** is any early release of the [Unix](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix "Unix") code base prior to Unix [System III](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_III "System III"), particularly the Research Unix releases prior to and including Version 7 (the base for [UNIX/32V](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNIX/32V "UNIX/32V") as well as later developments of [AT&T Unix](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T_Unix "AT&T Unix")).

## History

[AT&T](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T "AT&T") licensed Version 5 to educational institutions, and Version 6 also to commercial sites. Schools paid \$200 and others \$20,000, discouraging most commercial use, but Version 6 was the most widely used version into the 1980s. Research Unix versions are often referred to by the edition of the [manual](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_page "Man page") that describes them,^([\[1\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-fiedler198310-1)) because early versions and the last few were never officially released outside of Bell Labs, and grew organically. So, the first Research Unix would be the First Edition, and the last the Tenth Edition. Another common way of referring to them is as "Version *x* Unix" or "V*x* Unix", where *x* is the manual edition. All modern editions of Unix—excepting [Unix-like](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix-like "Unix-like") implementations such as [Coherent](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_(operating_system) "Coherent (operating system)"), [Minix](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minix "Minix"), and [Linux](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux "Linux")—derive from the 7th Edition.^([\[2\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-2))

Starting with the 8th Edition, versions of Research Unix had a close relationship to [BSD](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Software_Distribution "Berkeley Software Distribution"). This began by using 4.1cBSD as the basis for the 8th Edition. In a [Usenet](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usenet "Usenet") post from 2000, [Dennis Ritchie](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Ritchie "Dennis Ritchie") described these later versions of Research Unix as being closer to BSD than they were to [UNIX System V](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNIX_System_V "UNIX System V"),^([\[3\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-3)) which also included some BSD code:^([\[1\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-fiedler198310-1))

> Research Unix 8th Edition started from (I think) BSD 4.1c, but with enormous amounts scooped out and replaced by our own stuff. This continued with 9th and 10th. The ordinary user command-set was, I guess, a bit more BSD-flavored than SysVish, but it was pretty eclectic.

## Licensing

After the publication of the [Lions' book](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Commentary_on_the_UNIX_Operating_System "A Commentary on the UNIX Operating System"), work was undertaken to release earlier versions of the [codebase](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codebase "Codebase"). SCO first released the code under a limited educational license.^(\[*[citation needed](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*\])

Later, in January 2002, [Caldera International](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera_International "Caldera International") (later to become [SCO Group](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCO_Group "SCO Group") and made defunct) [relicensed](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_relicensing "Software relicensing") (but has not made available) several versions under the four-clause [BSD license](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSD_license "BSD license"), up to and including [Version 7 Unix](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Version_7_Unix "Version 7 Unix") ([UNIX/32V](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNIX/32V "UNIX/32V")).^([\[16\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-16)[\[17\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-Caldera-license.pdf-17)) As of 2022^([\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Research_Unix&action=edit)), there has been no widespread use of the code, but it can be used on emulator systems, and *Version 5 Unix* runs on the [Nintendo](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo "Nintendo") [Game Boy Advance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy_Advance "Game Boy Advance") using the [SIMH](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIMH "SIMH") [PDP-11](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDP-11 "PDP-11") [emulator](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emulator "Emulator").^([\[18\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-gbaunix-18)) *Version 6 Unix* provides the basis for the MIT [xv6](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xv6 "Xv6") teaching system, which is an update of that version to ANSI C and the x86 or RISC-V platform.

The [BSD](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Software_Distribution "Berkeley Software Distribution") [vi](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi_(text_editor) "Vi (text editor)") [text editor](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_editor "Text editor") is based on code from the [ed](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_(text_editor) "Ed (text editor)") line editor in those early Unixes. Therefore, "traditional" vi could not be distributed freely, and various work-alikes (such as [nvi](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvi "Nvi")) were created. Now that the original code is no longer encumbered, the "traditional" vi has been adapted for modern [Unix-like](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix-like "Unix-like") operating systems.^([\[19\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-19))

SCO Group, Inc. was previously called Caldera International. As a result of the *[SCO Group, Inc. v. Novell, Inc.](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCO_Group,_Inc._v._Novell,_Inc. "SCO Group, Inc. v. Novell, Inc.")* case, Novell, Inc. was found not to have transferred the copyrights of UNIX to SCO Group, Inc.^([\[20\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-20)) Concerns have been raised regarding the validity of the Caldera license.^([\[21\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-21)[\[22\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-22))

## The Unix Heritage Society

*The Unix Heritage Society* was founded by Warren Toomey.^([\[23\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-lwn.net/725297-23)[\[24\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-bsdnow.tv/476-24)) First edition Unix was restored to a usable state by a restoration team from the Unix Heritage Society in 2008. The restoration process started with paper listings of the source code which were in PDP-11 assembly language.^([\[25\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-25)[\[26\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-26))

## Legacy

In 2002, [Caldera International](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera_International "Caldera International") released^([\[27\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-27)) Unix V1, V2, V3, V4, V5, [V6](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Version_6_Unix "Version 6 Unix"), [V7](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Version_7_Unix "Version 7 Unix") on [PDP-11](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDP-11 "PDP-11") and Unix 32V on [VAX](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAX "VAX") as [FOSS](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open_source_software "Free and open source software") under a [permissive](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permissive_license "Permissive license") [BSD-like](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSD-license "BSD-license") [software license](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_license "Software license").^([\[28\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-28)[\[17\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-Caldera-license.pdf-17)[\[29\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-29))

In 2017, [The Unix Heritage Society](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Unix_Heritage_Society "The Unix Heritage Society") and [Alcatel-Lucent](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcatel-Lucent "Alcatel-Lucent") USA Inc., on behalf of itself and [Nokia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia "Nokia") [Bell Laboratories](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Laboratories "Bell Laboratories"), released V8, V9, and V10 under the condition that only non-commercial use was allowed, and that they would not assert copyright claims against such use.^([\[30\]](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Unix#cite_note-30))
